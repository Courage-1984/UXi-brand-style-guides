SOL INVICTUS

Driving Innovation. Powering the Future.

Competing in the Sasol Solar Challenge 2026

Sol Invictus is a collaborative innovation initiative that brings together students, academics, and industry partners from Belgium Campus iTversity, IMM Graduate School, Open Window Institute and CTU Training Solutions.

At its heart is SOL1, a high-performance solar-electric vehicle competing in the Sasol Solar Challenge 2026.

More than a race car, SOL1 serves as a platform for learning, innovation, and industry collaboration. Through the project, students gain practical exposure to emerging technologies while contributing to solutions that address real-world engineering challenges.

Advancing AI-Powered Mobility

From Data to Action

Success in solar racing is determined by more than speed.

Weather conditions, energy consumption, battery performance, and route strategy all influence the outcome.

Through Sol Invictus, students contribute to technologies that transform data into actionable insights.

A recent milestone saw the development of an AI-enabled telemetry and neural network solution that allows SOL1 and its support vehicle to communicate in real time, creating a connected mobility platform capable of supporting race strategy, efficiency, and safety.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Sol Invictus?

Sol Invictus is a collaborative solar engineering initiative competing in the Sasol Solar Challenge 2026. The project brings together students, academics and industry partners to design, build and optimise a high-performance solar vehicle while developing future-ready skills.

Why is Belgium Campus iTversity involved in the project?

Belgium Campus iTversity believes students learn best by applying their knowledge to real-world challenges. Sol Invictus provides an opportunity for students to gain practical experience in technology, innovation, and collaborative problem-solving.

How does Sol Invictus support Learning 2.0?

The project reflects the Learning 2.0 philosophy by allowing students to learn through hands-on experience, industry collaboration and multidisciplinary teamwork while contributing to a live engineering project.

Which technologies do students work with?

Students gain exposure to a range of industry-relevant technologies, including Artificial Intelligence (AI), Software Development, Data Analytics, IoT, Cloud Computing, Cybersecurity, Embedded Systems and Communications Technologies.

What skills do students gain through the project?

Students develop practical technical skills, project management experience, teamwork, leadership, problem-solving abilities, and exposure to professional industry workflows.

Which institutions participate in Sol Invictus?

The project is a collaboration between Belgium Campus iTversity, IMM Graduate School, Open Window Institute and CTU Training Solutions, supported by industry partners and mentors.

Do students from different disciplines collaborate on the project?

Yes. Sol Invictus brings together students with expertise in technology, engineering, design, communication, and innovation, creating a multidisciplinary environment that mirrors modern industry.

How does the project prepare students for industry?

By working on a real-world project with measurable outcomes, students gain practical experience, build professional networks, strengthen their portfolios, and develop skills that are highly valued by employers.

Why is the Sasol Solar Challenge important?

The Sasol Solar Challenge provides a platform for innovation, sustainable engineering, and practical problem-solving. It allows students to evaluate technologies and ideas in a challenging real-world environment.

How can I support or get involved with Sol Invictus?

There are several ways to become part of the journey, whether as a student, industry partner, sponsor, or supporter. You can follow the team's progress, connect with the partners involved, or explore opportunities to collaborate with the project.
