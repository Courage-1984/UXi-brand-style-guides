The 2026 AMC Conference did not just reflect on the state of the industry \- it set a binding mandate for its future. Culminating in the signing of a landmark five-point written declaration by the African Marketing Confederation, the Livingstone summit established concrete commitments for African brand sovereignty, ethical AI, and chartered professionalism.

Across three days, marketing leaders transitioned the conversation from seeking visibility to demonstrating measurable economic value. Delegates from across the continent established that while scale can be regional, execution must remain fiercely local. Together, the AMC community laid the groundwork to ensure that Africa's youth, women, and small enterprises actively shape the continent's brand economy, rather than merely observing it.

### **4\. RECAP SECTION**

**H2:** **Looking Back at the 2026 AMC Conference**

**Paragraph Text**: 

Across three days, the programme unpacked the practical realities of a rising Africa. Discussions moved beyond standard metrics, highlighting that the modern Chief Marketing Officer must evolve into an enterprise growth leader. Delegates explored how artificial intelligence makes local knowledge more valuable, how the creator economy has bypassed traditional media, and why consumer trust must be treated as a measurable dividend.

### **H3: Day 1 – Africa Rising in a Multipolar World**

*“Africa’s next great brands will not be built by copying what already works, but by creating what truly matters to African consumers.” — Mohamed Salah Ben Slamia, Founder of MUSE Agency*

Day 1 set an ambitious commercial tone, establishing that marketing must serve as a primary engine for continental economic growth. Opening addresses by Dr Matongo Matamwandi and Dr Lucy Nyundo framed the summit's directive, while Keynote Speaker Mamadou Bah challenged delegates to stop treating Africa as a passive emerging market and instead become demand architects. Attendees gained actionable clarity on removing localised infrastructure friction, supported by strategic insights from Shaduri Umutoniwase and Oriane Abouattier Canfrin on converting cultural capital into global competitive advantage. During *The Africa Rising Exchange* immersion session, facilitated by Helen McIntee Carlisle, industry leaders including Agrippa Mugwagwa and Misikir Mulugeta provided evidence-based models for executing with local precision across complex pan-African markets.

### **Day 2 – Leadership, Trust and the AI-Enabled Economy**

*“Until African balance sheets can price intangibles—trademarks, creative rights, brand equity—we will keep financing the lowest-margin end of our own economies. Marketing builds the asset; finance must learn to value it.” — Humphrey Chola, Lead: Strategic Communications at Agra*

Day 2 confronted the digital economy, equipping delegates with strategies to govern data trust, leverage AfCFTA trade corridors, and demonstrate tangible ROI on corporate balance sheets. Following an opening address by Hermawan Kartajaya, Keynote Speaker Dr Bolajoko Bayo-Ajayi and Kwabena Agyekum tackled ethical AI governance, setting the stage for Keynote Speaker Mwewa Besa to outline marketing's role in driving continental market access. The focus then shifted to practical application during the Solutions Studio Live immersion session, where Terushka Heuvel and Tim Ekandjo demonstrated actionable methodologies for managing algorithmic reputation and proving economic impact. Rounding out the agenda, Humphrey Chola highlighted the commercial value of intangible assets before Special Feature Speaker Mpumelelo Zondi explored creator-driven discovery \- proving that authentic local context remains the ultimate digital currency.

\[Day 2 Highlights Carousel\]

### **Day 3 – Africa Rising at the Global Marketing Table**

*“Transformation begins when we look beyond the obvious—beyond what the market is today, beyond what customers are asking for today, and towards what they will need tomorrow.” — Keynote Speaker Lorraine Mlenga, Strategy, Digital & Data Executive at Old Mutual*

The final day turned the lens toward global competitiveness, equipping attendees to build resilient, future-ready brand equity. Keynote Speaker Lorraine Mlenga delivered a compelling case for aligning sustainability and purpose with consumer trust, establishing that true brand transformation requires anticipating long-term continental needs. This strategic momentum carried directly into the closing plenary led by Keynote Speaker Euphrem Odilon Gbaguidi, alongside David Balikuddembe, Gillian Rusike, and Kwabena Agyekum. Together, delegates defined how African marketers must actively shape global commercial narratives in an AI-enabled era, setting the stage for Dr Matongo Matamwandi to officially deliver the continental leadership roadmap and declaration.
